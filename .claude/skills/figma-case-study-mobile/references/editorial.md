# Editing a desktop case study for phones

The desktop deck is written for a hiring manager or reviewer who sits down with it. The phone reader is scrolling between other things. They should leave knowing: what the problem was, what was learned, what was designed, and what it achieved. Everything else is supporting evidence that can live on desktop.

A useful target: **6–8 sections, readable in about a minute.** If the outline is longer, cut again.

## The spine

Most product case studies reduce to this sequence. Map the deck's slides onto it; slides that map to nothing are candidates to drop.

1. **Hero** — project name, one-line summary of the goal, role/team/tools as a compact grid, the cover visual (or its video). Plus the one-line "short version — full case study on desktop" note.
2. **Problem** — one sentence and the number that proves it (a funnel, a drop-off, a cost). Make the number visual if it's a funnel or comparison.
3. **Impact** — the outcome metrics. On desktop these often come later; on mobile pull them up to section 2 or 3, because many readers stop early and the outcome is the most persuasive thing in the deck.
4. **Insights** — the 2–3 findings that the design responds to.
5. **How might we** — if the deck has one, it's a great bridge; keep it verbatim or lightly tightened.
6. **Solution** — one card per touchpoint/flow step: short title, one sentence on *why* it's designed that way, one image.
7. **Learnings** — a short list, titles only or title + half a line.

Not every deck has all of these (a visual-design or brand project has no funnel). Adapt the spine; keep the principle: *claim → evidence → design → result*.

## Choosing insights

Decks often list 5–9 insights. Rank each on:

- **Has a hard number** (71% didn't see offers) → strong keep.
- **Drives a design decision shown later** (trust broke at checkout → price shown upfront in the final flow) → strong keep; this is the causal link the reader needs.
- **Surprising or counter-intuitive** → keep if room.
- **Restates another insight**, or is generic ("users want clarity", "users found it confusing") → drop or merge.

Keep 2–3, at most 4. Merge two related findings into one line rather than keeping both thin.

## Choosing screens and flows

- Show **final designs** of the **main flow**, one representative screen per step. Five touchpoints → five cards, each with its single best image.
- Drop: wireframes, lo-fi explorations, discarded concepts, component/spec sheets, multiple states of the same screen, asset sheets (e.g. a slide of gift-box illustrations).
- Keep an exploration or before/after **only if it is the argument** — e.g. "we tried a combo booking flow and dropped it because…" can be one sentence, usually without its image.
- When one slide shows the same screen in two states (before/after, closed/open sheet), prefer the state that shows the intervention; if both matter, keep the pair side by side in one image.
- A desktop slide that has three phones in a row reads poorly at phone width (each phone ends up ~100px). Either pick the most important one, or accept the row as a "flow overview" image and keep the explanation in text.

## Process content

Research plans, screener criteria, discussion guides, survey setup, affinity maps, journey maps, stakeholder lists, tool logos, timelines: cut. If method credibility matters, one line under the insights ("From a survey of N flight bookers and 12 interviews") does the job — only if the deck states those numbers.

## Rewriting copy

- Shorter sentences. One idea per sentence. Active voice.
- Replace jargon with plain words where meaning survives ("cross-sell intervention" → "hotel offer"), but keep the domain terms the project is about.
- Headings can carry the insight, not just the topic: "The offers existed. People just didn't see them." beats "Major Insights".
- Never change facts. Numbers, percentages, names and quoted text are copied exactly. Don't round 5–6% to 5%, don't add metrics the deck doesn't state.
- Keep the author's voice where it has one; don't make it sound like marketing.

## Worked example (Hotel Cross Sell, 17 slides → 7 sections)

| Desktop slide(s) | Mobile |
|---|---|
| 1 Cover (title, subtitle, role/team/tools, phone video) | Hero: phone video cropped to the screen, title, one-line goal, desktop note, 2×2 meta grid |
| 2 Business problem (30% browse, 5–6% book, bar) + solution statement | "The problem": one heading + 3-bar funnel built in CSS. Solution statement dropped (the solution section says it) |
| 3 Impact (+4% attach, +22% CTR) | "Impact", moved to section 3: two stat cards + one phone image |
| 4 Funnel analytics + research plan (3 steps, pop-ups) | Cut |
| 5 Major insights (9 cards) | "What research told us": 71%, 91%, and "trust broke at checkout" — the three with numbers or a direct design consequence |
| 6 Context, 7 Usability insights (6 annotated screens) | Cut; their conclusion lives in the HMW |
| 7 HMW question | Kept, tightened, as a blue quote card |
| 8 Planning the approach, 9 initial combo ideation | Cut |
| 10 Intervention flow overview (5 thumbnails) | Became the section's structure: "One offer, shown at five moments" |
| 11–15 One slide per touchpoint, with 4 annotated callouts each | Five cards: title, one sentence distilled from the callouts, one image |
| 16 Learnings (4 cards) | Four one-line items |
| 17 Asset/exploration sheet | Cut |
