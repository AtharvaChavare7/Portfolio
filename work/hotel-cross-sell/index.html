Discussion Guide:

1. Ice breaker question (1min):

  • Tell me about yourself.

2. Early questions (15 min):

  • How often do you travel by flight in a year?
  • For what all purposes have you travelled by flight? (If tells some other trips, ask about leisure, pilgrimage, business)
  • Who do you usually travel with?
  • Name a few destinations you have been to by flight?
  • Could you tell me briefly about your trip planning process?
  • How long before the travel date do you usually book flight tickets?
  • When do you do the stay bookings? (Pick a destination from previous question)

3. Target questions (15 min):

  • You seem to have booked flights on MMT but not any hotels for those flight trips, why is that?
  • How else do you book hotels? If online, which apps do you use to book stays? Why? If offline, how do you book stays?

3. Closing questions (15 min):

  • Could you share your screen and show us your booking process?
  • Any suggestions or improvements on how we can make the experience better for you?

Graduation project · 2024–25

Hotel Cross Sell

Getting flight bookers on MakeMyTrip to book their stay too.

You’re reading the short version. Open on a desktop for the full case study.

Role
Product Designer & Researcher
Team
CPO, PMs, Data Analysts, User Researchers
Tools
Figma, Adobe AE, Optimal Workshop
Focus
Cross-selling

The problem

Lots of people book flights. Very few book a stay with them.

100% book a flight
30% browse stays
5–6% book a stay

Impact

Hotel offers at the right moments in the flight journey.

+4%hotel attach rate among flight bookers
+22%click-through on cross-sell offers

What research told us

The offers existed. People just didn’t see them, or didn’t trust them.

  • 71%didn’t see any hotel offer after booking a flight, even though offers were there.
  • 91%said yes or maybe to flight and hotel offers together.
  • Trustbroke when the price went up at checkout.
How might we make hotel offers visible, easy to understand, and worth acting on at the right moments?

The solution

One offer, shown at five moments.

  1. 01

    During flight booking

    A light “15% off your next hotel” strip plants the idea without interrupting the booking.

  2. 02

    Thank you screen

    The hotel card sits above the booking details, made for a 3–4 second glance.

  3. 03

    Bottom sheet

    A gift that opens frames the offer as a reward, not an ad.

  4. 04

    Home screen

    The code is pre-applied and the button is tied to the trip: “Explore stays in Goa”.

  5. 05

    Search to checkout

    The offer follows you through every screen, with savings shown upfront and no coupon to apply.

What I learned

  • Explaining design decisions to a wide group of stakeholders
  • Using business metrics to guide design
  • Designing within engineering and timeline constraints
  • Making decisions and owning the trade-offs
